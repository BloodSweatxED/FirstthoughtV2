# Memo: proposal for a randomized pilot in Becoming a Physician

**To:** Sandra Oza, Course Director, Becoming a Physician
**From:** [Investigator]
**Date:** [date]
**Re:** A four minute differential diagnosis exercise, run as a randomized pilot

---

## What I am asking for

Permission to run a small teaching exercise in half the Becoming a Physician
small groups this block, and to compare the two halves on something the course
already measures.

Six minutes per session. Sixteen to twenty sessions. Under two hours of course
time across the whole block, per student.

## What the exercise is

Students see a chief complaint and almost nothing else. A patient presents with
chest pain. That is all they get. They have four minutes to write a differential
on their phone, unaided.

Then the room sees the aggregate. How many students named each dangerous
diagnosis. Which organ systems nobody thought of. One teaching point. The
facilitator runs a short debrief.

The point is the discomfort of reasoning with too little information, which is
what an actual first encounter feels like and what students almost never
practice.

## Why it might be worth the time

Students are usually asked to reason after being handed a complete case. The
skill of generating a differential from a bare complaint is assumed rather than
taught. This gives them roughly twenty repetitions of exactly that, with
immediate feedback on what the group missed.

Whether it works is an open question. That is why I want to run it as a
randomized comparison rather than simply adding it to the course.

## What it is not

It is not an AI tool. It contains no language model and makes no external calls.
It scores what students write against a list of diagnosis names that three
emergency physicians will review and sign off before anything runs.

It never writes a differential for the student. The student has to produce one
first, alone, before the tool shows anything at all. If there is a concern that
digital tools do students' thinking for them, this is built the other way round
on purpose.

## The design

Half the small groups run the exercise. Half continue as usual. Groups are
assigned by chance, balanced across MS1 and MS2, so 22 groups in each arm.

The comparison is the differential diagnosis section of the post-encounter note
on the end-of-block OSCE, which the course already collects and already reviews.
No new assessment, no new testing time.

With both class years included, this is large enough to detect a realistic
effect. With one class year it would not be, which is why I am asking for both.

## What it costs the course

- Six minutes per session in half the groups
- One ten minute written task for every student before the block starts
- A short training session for facilitators, and a one page script
- Nothing from the assessment schedule

## What protects the students

- The exercise is never graded and cannot affect standing in any way
- Students are identified only by a study number, and I never see the list that
  connects numbers to names. The assessment office holds it.
- Submissions go through Qualtrics, which the institution already runs
- All cases are synthetic. No patient information is involved.
- Students may decline to have their data used and still take part in the
  activity with their group
- I will not deliver the exercise to any group and will not score any outcome

I will seek IRB determination before collecting anything.

## What the course gets either way

If it works, you have evidence for a cheap addition that fits in existing
sessions.

If it does not work, you have a clean answer about a teaching idea that sounds
appealing, and the exercise stops. Both outcomes are useful and the second one
costs the course under two hours per student.

You also get a per-session picture of where the class's reasoning is thin. If
two thirds of the room never mentions aortic dissection on a chest pain case,
that is worth knowing in January rather than at the OSCE.

4. Anyone else who needs to agree before I go to the IRB

Happy to walk through it in person, or to run one session with you so you can
