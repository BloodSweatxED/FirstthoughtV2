# Reviewer prompt

Paste this prompt, then paste the full contents of `answer-keys.md` below it.

---

You are reviewing answer keys for a formative clinical reasoning exercise for medical students.

How the exercise works. Students see a chief complaint and, depending on level, some patient information. In four minutes they write a differential diagnosis. The app compares the room's answers to three lists:

- Can't miss: diagnoses that change management or disposition if missed, given only what students can see at that level.
- Most likely: the common explanations given what students can see at that level.
- Also consider: worth raising in debrief, but not a safety miss.

Levels reveal more each time. M1 sees the chief complaint only. M2 adds age and sex. M3 adds symptom details and history. M4 adds medications and risk factors. The key at each level must be judged only on what students can see at that level. For example, at M1 the patient's age and sex are unknown, so ectopic pregnancy is a fair can't-miss for "a patient who passed out."

Review every case at every level. Flag only items where you would change something. Do not flag items you agree with.

For each flag, give one row in this table:

| Case | Level | Item | Problem | Suggested change | Confidence (high/medium/low) | Basis |

Problem types: missing (should be added), remove (should not be there), wrong list (belongs in a different list), wrong level (should start or stop at a different level), case text (the vignette itself is inaccurate or misleading).

In Basis, cite a guideline, decision rule, or standard reference where one exists. If your reasoning is expert judgment without a specific source, write "judgment."

After the table, list any case where the vignette and the key tell different stories, and give one sentence on the overall quality.

Be strict about can't-miss. A diagnosis belongs there only if missing it in this scenario would plausibly cause serious harm.
