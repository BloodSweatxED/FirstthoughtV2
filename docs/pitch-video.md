# Pitch video and supporting material

The video replaces most of the slide deck. A committee that watches six students do the exercise does not need it described.

Target length: 3 to 4 minutes. Under 3 feels thin. Over 4 loses the room.

## One-sentence pitch

Four minutes, one chief complaint, every student answers, and the room sees what it caught and what it missed.

## Storyboard

| Time | Picture | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 to 0:20 | Black screen with the chief complaint in white: "A patient presents with chest pain." Then a phone clock counting down. | You, voice only: "This is all a first-year student gets. Four minutes. What could this be?" |
| 0:20 to 0:45 | Wide shot of the room. Students scan the QR code. Close shot of hands typing. | Room sound. One line of voiceover: the problem. Students wait for more history before they commit. Cold-calling one student exposes that student and tells you nothing about the other five. |
| 0:45 to 1:15 | Screen recording: the count goes from 0 to 6. The clock runs down. Cut to faces. | Voiceover: every student answers. No names. Nobody sees another answer until the reveal. |
| 1:15 to 2:00 | The reveal. Screen recording of the two lists and the red line. Cut to you teaching from it. | Live audio of you reading "Nobody named ..." and asking why it matters. This is the center of the video. Let it run. |
| 2:00 to 2:20 | Close shot of a phone showing "How you compare". | Voiceover: each student sees their own answer against the room. Only they see it. |
| 2:20 to 2:45 | Screen recording of round 2 at a higher level. The patient details appear. The lists change. | Voiceover: the same case scales from first year to fourth. More information changes what counts as dangerous. |
| 2:45 to 3:15 | The recap page. Hold on the headline for 4 seconds. | Voiceover: the real numbers from this session. What the room caught. What the typical student caught alone. |
| 3:15 to 3:45 | Two student reactions, one sentence each. | Their words. |
| 3:45 to 4:00 | Title card: First Thought. The ask in one line. | You: the ask. |

## What to say and what to leave out

Say:

- It takes four minutes and no prep. It fits at the start of a session that already exists.
- The cases are synthetic. The app stores no names, emails, or device identifiers.
- Matching uses a curated dictionary that faculty can read and correct. It shows what it could not read.
- The answer keys were reviewed by two physicians. Only say this once step 2 of the roadmap is done.
- The numbers on screen are from this one session with these six students.

Leave out, or the committee will fix on it:

- Any claim that students improved. One session cannot show that.
- The word "study" unless there is an approved protocol.
- "AI grading." Nothing is graded and the live path has no AI model in it.

## The ask

Decide this before the edit. The last line of the video should be one of these:

- Approve a pilot in BAP next term, with a set number of sessions.
- Name a faculty partner for a second site.
- Support an IRB submission to measure change over repeated sessions.

## One-page summary

Hand this out or send it ahead. One side of one page:

1. The problem, in two sentences.
2. What First Thought is, in two sentences, with one screenshot of the reveal.
3. What happened in the filmed session: rounds, answers, different diagnoses named, what the room caught, what the typical learner caught alone. All from the recap page.
4. What it costs: no license fee, no student accounts, about 30 minutes of setup.
5. Privacy: what is stored and what is not.
6. The ask.

## Backup slides

Three slides, in case the video cannot play:

1. The chief complaint and the four-minute clock.
2. A screenshot of the reveal.
3. A screenshot of the recap.

Then run the live fallback from the roadmap: fill a sample room and click Reveal.

## Editing notes

- Cut on the reveal. The moment the lists appear is the strongest picture you have.
- Use the screen recording full frame. Small text in a corner box cannot be read from the back of a room.
- Add captions. Committee rooms have bad speakers.
- Blur or crop any student who opted out.
- Do not add music under people talking.
